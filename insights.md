## Insight 1: City Inequality — Some Cities Breathe Twice as Bad

**Ahmedabad** has the highest average AQI of **388.3**,
which falls in the *Poor* category.

## Insight 2: The Most Polluted City Spends 80.5% of Days in Unhealthy Air

**Ahmedabad** spent **80.5%** of all days in the dataset at Poor, Very Poor,
Severe, or Severe+ AQI levels. That means residents of Ahmedabad experience
health-damaging air for more than 80% of the year on average — 
not just occasional spikes, but a persistent and serious problem.

## Insight 3: Air Quality Has Improved Over 5 Years

The national average AQI was **211.4** in 2015 and
**112.7** in 2020 — a change of 98.7 points.
This improvement is encouraging, though AQI levels remain high in many cities.

## Insight 4: Winter Is the Most Polluted Season — Nearly 1.9× Worse Than Monsoon

Average AQI in **Winter** is **210.7** versus just **113.2**
in **Monsoon**. The worst individual month is **Nov**
(avg AQI 229.4) and the best is **Jul**
(avg AQI 110.9).
Cold, still winter air traps pollutants close to the ground; monsoon rains wash
them away, which is why Monsoon is cleaner.

## Insight 5: PM2.5 Is the Strongest Predictor of Bad AQI (r = 0.732)

Of all 12 pollutants measured, **PM2.5** has the highest correlation
with AQI (Pearson r = **0.732**), followed closely by **PM10**
(r = 0.572). A correlation near 1.0 means these pollutants rise and fall
almost in lockstep with the overall AQI score. Reducing PM2.5 
emissions — from vehicles, industry, and crop burning — would have the
biggest measurable impact on air quality.
